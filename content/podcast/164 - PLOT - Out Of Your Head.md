# 164 - PLOT - Out Of Your Head

**Length:** 20 min (~3,000 words). The six stage walk is the block to trim if you want 15.

**Belief shift**
They think: systems are a big office job. Folders, procedures, nine page Word docs. Something you do later, when you're bigger.
Actually: your business already runs on systems. Every one of them is stored in the only place you cannot back up, cannot delegate, and cannot sell. Your head.
It breaks when: they realise they did not fail at writing systems last time. They failed at keeping them alive, and so did you.

2. "Don't ring me. Ring the folder. No, mate, the other folder."
3. "Righto, everyone stop. Who moved the thing where the thing goes?"

Then, word for word: **"Welcome to Simplified Sparky Marketing."**

Number 1 is the pick. It sounds like a gag for about two seconds and then it does not.

## [0:20] WHERE THIS CAME FROM (~40s)

- I have been rebuilding something I got certified in five years ago, and going back through my own old work has been humbling.
- I found systems I spent months writing that I have never once opened.
- Say it plainly: I am not going to sit here and tell you I nailed this. I got this wrong for years, expensively, and I want to tell you exactly how.

## [1:00] THE TEST (~2 min)

Do not soften this. It is the hook and it has to sting.

- "Someone rings you tomorrow. Dream job, three months, starts Monday, best money you've ever been offered. But you cannot touch your business while you're on it. Could you go?"
- Then the smaller version, because the big one lets them off the hook: **"Forget three months. Could you take three weeks off right now without the phone melting?"**
- And the killer: "When was the last holiday where you didn't quote from the pool?"
- Let it sit. Do not rescue them. This is the whole episode in one question.
- The line: **your business does not work. You work, and everything else watches you do it.**

---

## [3:00] THE STORY: how I got this wrong (~5 min)

This is the most valuable five minutes of the episode. Be specific and do not clean it up.

- **2021.** I paid for a certification in a proper systemisation method. Not a YouTube video, the real thing. Did the work, sat the case studies.
- I then consulted on real electrical businesses with it. Name them if you're comfortable: **IMPACT, Inner West, TEG.**
- Then I ran the full method on my own business, Westcork. Mapped the whole thing out, homeowner, ceiling fans, marketing all the way through to repeat work. Every box linked to a written procedure.
- **Say how long it took. Months.** And say honestly that it worked, for a while. Onboarding got easier. Things got consistent.
- Then the confession, and this is the beat that earns you the episode:
  - The procedures lived in a wiki nobody opened. Up a ladder, asking a mate is faster than logging into software.
  - They were written long, office style. Sparkies learn by watching, not reading.
  - The business changed. The documents did not. And the first time somebody opens a doc that's out of date, they stop trusting all of them.
  - Nobody owned them. No habit to keep them alive.
- The specific one that still makes you wince: **the hot water element SOP.** Built it properly. Never used it. Six, seven, eight years.
- Land it: **"I didn't fail at writing systems. I failed at keeping them alive."**

## [8:00] THE TURN - say this

**"And here's what took me five years to work out. That wasn't me being slack. That is the standard result. The method was never built for a business our size."**

## [8:20] WHY EVERY SPARKY'S ATTEMPT DIES (~2.5 min)

- Most systemisation projects that people start themselves are dead inside about 90 days. **Attribute it loosely, say the mob who wrote the method reckon, do not state it as a hard fact on a public feed.**
- Why ours die specifically: it assumes you have office staff. It assumes a spare person to be the one who writes it all up. It assumes a team that reads documents. It assumes a subscription to a wiki.
- Who in a five man sparky crew has a spare office person sitting around writing procedures? Nobody. Not one.
- So the thinking was right and the delivery was 2020. **That's not a small distinction. That's the whole reason it never stuck for us.**
- The one liner: **you're the bottleneck, not the librarian.**
- And the bit that changes it: the job nobody was ever going to do, the typing, is the exact job AI is now good at. You talk. It writes. That is the unlock, and it did not exist when I got certified.

---

## [10:50] THE SIX STAGES (~7 min)

Tease each one. Roughly a minute each. You are giving them the map, not the build.

Frame first: **"It's a wheel, not a ladder. Six stages, and the last one feeds back into the first. That's on purpose."**

**1. Money Flow.** The path a dollar takes through your business, from a stranger noticing you to a client coming back. You only systemise what sits on that path. Ten to fifteen systems, not hundreds. This is the bit that kills the "I'd have to document everything" excuse dead.

**2. The Who.** Put an honest name against every box. Most blokes do this and find one name in every single box. Theirs. That's the first time they see key person dependency written in their own handwriting. The line: **"your name should be on this map less than anybody's. Right now it's on all of it."**

**3. Construct.** Talk, don't type. You do the job and you talk while you do it, the AI writes it up. Nobody starts from a blank page, you're only ever marking up a draft, and every tradie on earth would rather fix someone else's work than write their own. Ten minutes of talking per system.

**4. House.** A system nobody can find is a system that doesn't exist. This is where mine died. One home, one page each, and it has to live where the work happens. On the job card, on the phone, laminated in the van if that's what it takes. Not a wiki.

**5. The Brain.** Once they're all in one place, AI sits on top of the lot and becomes the second brain of the business. The "how do I" questions that hit your phone at 7am hit that instead, and it answers your way, out of your systems, not generic rubbish off the internet. **"Imagine your worst Monday. Seven missed calls. Every one of those questions already answered before they thought to ring you."**

**6. Maintain.** The stage the entire industry skips, and the reason for the graveyard. Fifteen minutes a week. What went sideways this week, which system let it happen, fix the step. And the mindset inside it: stop asking who stuffed up, start asking what part of the system let it happen. Fix the system and it never happens again with anyone. Blame the bloke and you'll have the same conversation next month with somebody new.

Then close the wheel: **"Maintain feeds straight back into Money Flow. That's why it's a circle. It was never a project with a finish line. It's maintenance, same as test and tag."**

---

## [17:50] THE ONE THING TO GO AND DO

"Don't go and build anything. Just do this. For five days, write down every time somebody has to ask you something before they can keep working. Every text, every call, every 'mate, quick one'. Don't fix any of it. Just count them. That list is your business, sitting outside your head for the first time."

That's what we're working through with the members right now, stage by stage. If you want a hand with it, all the links to find me are in the description below."

## Before you record - three checks

**1. Do not repeat Ep 100.** You already did "your life is a system, you brush your teeth the same way, going to the toilet is a system" in October 2025. It was funny and it landed. If you do it again the regulars will clock it. This episode earns its place by being the honest failure story and the map, not the "everything is a system" bit.

**2. The VA is half spent.** Ep 86 already told the missed calls and the VA story. The angle that is still fresh is "she runs on systems, not experience, and every question she asked exposed a gap". One line only, do not retell the whole thing.

**3. Naming the method.** Naming Gerber and The E-Myth is completely safe, it's forty years old and famous. Naming the certification you did while publicly selling your rebuilt version of it is a judgement call and it's yours to make. The safe wording is: "I got certified in a proper method for this back in 2021, paid for it, did the work." Credits it honestly, names nobody, keeps you clean.

## Two things to sort before this becomes a series

**The stage names have drifted.** Your presenter notes from 25 August say Overview, Money Flow, Extract, House, Deploy, Service. Your cheat sheets from the next day say Who, Construct, House, Brain, Maintain. The 8 September session locks in Who and Construct. I have plotted the newer set. Confirm it, because if you say "Extract" and "Deploy" on a public feed and the members are looking at "Construct" and "Brain", that's a credibility ding for no reason.

**Deploy has vanished.** In the old six, Deploy was the stage that killed the "my team won't follow them" myth. Toolbox talk, every system gets an owner. In the new six it's gone and The Brain has taken the slot. That myth is the number one objection from anyone with staff, so where does it get answered now? If the answer is "inside The Who, under who should own it", fine, say so on air. If it doesn't get answered anywhere, that's a hole in the model and better to find it now than on a live call.

## On doing all six as a series

You said you want a podcast on each stage. Worth thinking about before you commit: six episodes that teach all six stages is the whole product given away for free, and the podcast's job is to leave a gap, not close it.

The shape that works: each of the six gets an episode, but each one gives the **what** and the **why** in full and holds the **how**. One episode per stage on the problem it solves and what it costs you not to have it. The build stays inside. That way the series makes people want in rather than making them feel sorted.
